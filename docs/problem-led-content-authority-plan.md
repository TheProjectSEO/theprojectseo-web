# Problem-led content and authority plan

Last updated: 2026-07-29

## Executive decision

TheProjectSEO will not build its editorial strategy around phrases such as “best SEO agency.” Those prompts represent a late-stage provider-selection decision. They remain useful for visibility monitoring, comparison-page quality, and conversion research, but they do not describe how most buyers first experience the problem.

The publishing system starts with an expensive or frustrating business problem:

1. A buyer recognizes a symptom.
2. A problem-led page provides a direct diagnosis.
3. A visual, dataset, worksheet, or tool helps the buyer inspect the problem.
4. A case study or method shows how the diagnosis was applied.
5. The relevant service page explains implementation, qualification, pricing, and the next step.

The article earns attention by solving the problem. The commercial page converts the reader when implementation help becomes necessary.

## How this changes the previous coverage-gap research

The previous research identified commercially relevant platform and industry gaps, including Next.js, WordPress, WooCommerce, Magento, Webflow, headless systems, BPO, manufacturing, logistics, hotels, recruitment, and construction.

Those findings remain candidate market owners. They are not approval to create a large taxonomy of near-identical “SEO agency for X” pages.

Each platform or industry must first prove:

- A recurring operator problem
- A commercially relevant search journey
- A page format that is not already owned by an existing page
- Access to credible evidence
- A useful visual or functional asset
- A relevant service and conversion path

Example:

| Taxonomy-first idea | Problem-led entry point | Commercial owner |
|---|---|---|
| Best Next.js SEO agency | Why deployed Next.js pages render for users but disappear from search | Technical SEO or a validated Next.js service page |
| Best SaaS SEO agency | Why organic traffic does not become qualified demos | `/industries/saas` |
| Best hotel SEO agency | Where the direct-booking journey leaks to OTAs | `/industries/travel` |
| Best Shopify SEO agency | Why collection pages fail to own category demand | `/seo-for-shopify` |
| Best BPO SEO agency | What buyers need to verify before shortlisting an outsourcing provider | A validated BPO commercial page |
| Best logistics SEO agency | How to cover lanes, locations, and services without doorway pages | A validated logistics commercial page |

## The page system

### Problem page

Answers the first question a buyer asks and names the likely causes.

Examples:

- Why are impressions increasing while product-page clicks fall?
- Why are hotel guests discovering the property but booking through an OTA?
- Why is SaaS organic traffic producing students and job seekers instead of qualified demos?
- Why is a brand visible in Google but absent from AI-generated vendor shortlists?

### Diagnostic or tool

Lets the reader inspect the problem without immediately hiring an agency.

Examples:

- Query-to-demo cohort worksheet
- Direct-booking leakage audit
- Collection-page indexability lab
- Migration risk register
- AI source-eligibility checklist

### Evidence page

Shows the setup, source, date, observation, limitation, action, and outcome.

Examples:

- Expressway.PH low-authority discovery study
- Template-level crawl and indexation experiment
- Qualified-demo attribution cohort
- OTA-versus-direct journey comparison

### Commercial page

Explains who the service fits, what work is included, what evidence is required, how implementation works, what it costs, and what happens after the form.

The commercial page does not need to repeat the entire problem resource. It links to the supporting evidence and focuses on the buying decision.

## User-friendly page pattern

Every major resource should follow this sequence:

1. Short H1 naming the problem
2. One-paragraph direct answer
3. Visual diagnosis or evidence snapshot
4. Symptoms and likely causes
5. A worksheet, calculator, comparison, or decision tree
6. Real evidence or clearly labeled example data
7. What the reader can fix internally
8. When specialist implementation is justified
9. Contextual link to the relevant service
10. Short form or CTA that carries the page and problem context

The page should not delay the answer until after a long definition, company story, or generic description of SEO.

## What makes a page worth linking to

People rarely link to a service pitch because another page needs evidence, utility, or a source—not another agency claim.

Prioritize these linkable assets:

### Original research

- Anonymized datasets
- Longitudinal observations
- Search-result or AI-response studies
- Template-level crawl experiments
- Industry benchmarks with a published method
- Before-and-after evidence with limitations

### Reusable tools

- Calculators
- Audit worksheets
- Decision trees
- Checklists with evidence fields
- Public templates
- Schema, redirect, or migration validators

### Reference resources

- Maintained definitions
- Versioned platform-change logs
- Source and crawler documentation maps
- Public taxonomies
- Industry-specific measurement dictionaries

### Expert evidence

- Practitioner commentary attached to a real event or dataset
- Technical explanations reporters can quote
- Approved client lessons
- Reproducible implementation examples

### Visual assets

- Original charts
- Architecture diagrams
- Annotated screenshots
- Process maps
- Comparison tables

Every chart must expose its source, date, unit, scope, and limitation. Embeddable assets should provide an accessible description and a stable source URL.

## A Wikipedia-safe authority path

### The objective

Wikipedia is not a directory or a link-building platform. A Wikipedia citation or external link is an editorial outcome, not a deliverable we can promise.

Wikipedia's external-link guideline says links must be minimal, meritable, directly relevant, and useful to readers. It also advises people to avoid adding links to sites they own, maintain, or represent. Promotional links and pages that primarily sell products or services are normally avoided:

`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:External_links`

Wikipedia also prefers published reliable sources and relies mainly on secondary sources. Self-published sources are largely unacceptable, with limited exceptions:

`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources`

### The legitimate route

1. Identify an encyclopedic claim, dataset, historical record, technical definition, or topic where better sourcing is genuinely needed.
2. Publish a stable, neutral, accessible source with a transparent method, author, date, references, corrections policy, and no gated access.
3. Seek independent review or coverage from a publication with editorial control and a reputation for fact-checking.
4. Let independent editors decide whether the original research or secondary coverage improves a Wikipedia article.
5. If TheProjectSEO has a conflict of interest, disclose it and propose the source on the article's Talk page rather than inserting the link directly.
6. Accept rejection or removal without repeatedly replacing the link.

Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest guideline strongly discourages direct editing of affected articles and provides a Talk-page process for proposing changes:

`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest`

Anyone paid or expected to be paid for a Wikipedia contribution must disclose the employer, client, and affiliation:

`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Paid-contribution_disclosure`

### What we will not do

- Buy a Wikipedia link
- Hire an undisclosed editor
- Create an article about TheProjectSEO to obtain an official-site link
- Replace dead links with a commercially convenient page
- Insert TheProjectSEO into lists of agencies
- Add links from multiple accounts
- Treat a nofollow Wikipedia link as a ranking shortcut
- Measure the program by the number of Wikipedia URLs obtained

### Content with the best chance of becoming reference-worthy

TheProjectSEO's strongest candidates are not agency pages:

- A reproducible AI-visibility measurement methodology
- A public dataset on source and citation variability
- The Expressway.PH low-authority visibility study, after first-party verification
- A maintained technical reference covering crawler access and source eligibility
- A transparent study of direct-booking search leakage
- A versioned migration-risk dataset

Independent editorial coverage is more important than the mere existence of the source on TheProjectSEO.

## How to earn links from other sites

### Research-led digital PR

Create a defensible result with a strong, narrow finding. Give reporters and industry publications:

- The finding in one sentence
- The method and sample
- A chart they can inspect
- The raw or aggregated data where privacy permits
- A named practitioner available for questions
- The limitation

Do not distribute a press release whose only claim is that TheProjectSEO launched a service.

### Expert commentary

Respond to relevant journalist, publisher, podcast, and newsletter requests with an answer that can stand without a company link. The reference becomes natural when the publisher needs the practitioner's biography, data, or method.

### Resource inclusion

Find resource pages, implementation guides, curricula, documentation, and industry playbooks already covering the task. Recommend a specific asset only when it fills a visible gap or replaces a genuinely broken source.

### Partner and vendor education

Create joint implementation material with developers, analytics providers, ecommerce implementers, hotel technology companies, CRM consultants, and industry specialists. Each partner must contribute expertise or distribution. A page created only to exchange links is not acceptable.

### Unlinked mention reclamation

Monitor mentions of TheProjectSEO, Aditya Aman, approved case studies, datasets, charts, and tools. Request a source link when the publisher already relied on the work and a link improves attribution for the reader.

### Case-study participation

When software, analytics, hosting, or implementation partners document a real use case, provide verified evidence and an approved explanation. Do not manufacture a testimonial or partner story merely for a backlink.

### Industry associations and education

Contribute practical research, templates, webinars, or training where the association's audience benefits. Sponsorship alone does not justify a followed editorial link.

### Podcasts, newsletters, and events

Pitch a specific lesson, experiment, or data finding. Avoid generic topics such as “the future of SEO.” Publish a stable source page so the host can link to the evidence rather than only the homepage.

## Link prospecting method

For each new asset:

1. Inspect the referring domains and exact referring pages of comparable assets.
2. Separate live editorial references from scrapes, archives, profiles, redirects, and spam.
3. Identify the reason each credible page linked.
4. Group prospects by audience and editorial job: citation, tool, example, data, expert, or partner.
5. Write outreach around the missing value on the prospect's page.
6. Record contact, relationship, pitch, response, placement, qualification, target, and status.
7. Review the referring page after publication and again after 90 days.

Domain Rating does not determine whether a link is valuable. Page relevance, editorial reason, placement, audience, indexation, longevity, and target-page fit matter more.

## Outreach standard

An outreach message must answer:

- Why this specific page was selected
- Which reader problem the asset helps solve
- What is original about the asset
- Which claim or resource it could improve
- Where the source and methodology can be inspected

Do not use:

- Mass guest-post pitches
- Purchased followed links
- Automated forum or profile links
- Excessive reciprocal-link arrangements
- Optimized-anchor requirements
- Fake experts or fabricated statistics
- “We noticed you linked to X, so link to us” without a substantive improvement

Google defines links created primarily to manipulate rankings as link spam. Its examples include paid ranking links, excessive exchanges, automated link creation, and advertorial links that pass ranking credit:

`https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies`

## Conversion design

Each problem-led resource maps to one commercial owner. The CTA names the implementation problem rather than asking the reader to “grow” or “unlock results.”

Examples:

- “Audit which search journeys produce accepted demos”
- “Find where direct bookings leak to OTAs”
- “Review collection indexation and revenue ownership”
- “Map the source gaps behind AI vendor shortlists”

The form stores:

- Source resource
- Problem selected
- Website
- Business email
- Service or implementation context
- Plan interest
- Attribution fields

The follow-up should reference the problem and evidence the person used, not send a generic agency introduction.

## Measurement

### Resource quality

- Relevant organic queries
- Tool or worksheet use
- Scroll and evidence interaction
- Return visits
- Corrections and expert feedback

### Authority

- Verified editorial referring pages
- New referring domains by audience and reason
- Citations to original data
- Unlinked mentions
- Independent coverage
- Links retained after 90 and 180 days

### Commercial impact

- Assisted commercial-page visits
- Form starts and completions
- Accepted company leads
- Discovery calls
- Opportunities and revenue

Do not count spam domains, scraper links, mirrors, or unverified domain-level rows as authority wins.

## Publication gate

No resource enters production until it has:

- One named buyer and problem
- Evidence of recurrence
- A clear page owner
- A live SERP corpus
- Primary or first-party sources
- An information-gain asset
- A visual plan
- A linkable reason
- A conversion role
- A measurement definition
- A named reviewer
- A refresh trigger
